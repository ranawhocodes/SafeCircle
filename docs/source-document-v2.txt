Problem statement (as given):
Accelerating Emergency Response and Enhancing Safety Accessibility Across
Communities. During critical safety incidents and localized emergencies, residents in
residential neighborhoods and public spaces face severe friction in initiating timely
calls for help and obtaining actionable situational updates. Traditional reporting
channels are often fragmented, causing delays in dispatching local authorities,
coordinating nearby volunteers, and providing clear status updates to those
affected. The issue is further exacerbated for vulnerable demographics, such as
children, senior citizens, and individuals with physical or cognitive disabilities, who
may find navigating complex smartphone applications difficult or impossible during
high-stress scenarios. When an emergency strikes, relying exclusively on app-based
reporting excludes these groups from receiving rapid, accessible assistance.
The idea in one paragraph: a child, senior or person with a disability presses one physical
button, or a family member sends "HELP" on WhatsApp. Within seconds the system
knows who they are and where they are: the registered address at home, or the Go
pendant's GPS outside. It alerts the nearest verified volunteers, the society guard and the
family at the same time, and escalates to 112 if nobody responds. The button tells the
person, by light, sound and vibration, that help is coming. Volunteers arrive already
knowing who they are helping and how.
One-line pitch: "112 gets the police there. We make sure the person is never alone, and
never phone-dependent, in the minutes before they arrive."
Deck line: "No app for victims. A responder app for volunteers."

2. The two-faced solution at a glance
SafeCircle has two faces joined by one backend. Face A serves the person in trouble and
their family with no app at all; Face B is an app for the people who respond.

2.2 End-to-end flow
1. Setup (once): caregiver pairs the button, fills the profile and runs a drill (Section 5).
2. Trigger: hold the button, send "HELP", or a stranger scans the pendant QR (Section 4).
3. Identify and score: the backend finds the person, locates them (registered address, or
GPS and Wi-Fi from the pendant), merges duplicates and sets severity (Section 8).
4. Alert in tiers: 3 nearest verified volunteers get a high-priority alarm, everyone else a
normal alert, guard and family at the same time; all upgraded at 30 s; 112 at 90 s or at
once for P1 (Section 9).
5. Accept and go: fresh location, full briefing, smart flashcards, live status taps (Sections
10, 11, 14).
6. Feedback: the button shows each stage; the family page updates (Sections 7, 15).
7. Cancel path: a long-press cancel triggers a verification call; no answer keeps the alert
alive (Section 6).
8. Close and learn: outcome logged, profile history updated, volunteer wellbeing check
(Sections 8, 10).
2.3 Core differentiators
1. Phone-free trigger for people who cannot use an app, at home or outside (GPS
pendant).
2. Tiered, verified-first alerting that reaches everyone at once but wakes the closest
trusted people hardest.
3. Cancel with verification: a forced cancel does not end help.
4. Status the person can feel: light, sound and vibration for each stage.
5. Profile-aware dispatch and smart flashcards: responders know who they are helping
and what they will probably find.
6. Community-vouched trust: neighbours who know a volunteer approve them.
7. Battery-aware responder location: exact position only when someone nearby needs
help.

3. Research: existing systems, prior art and gaps
India's 112 system already accepts IoT and external signals, and proven volunteer-alert
apps abroad use the same model we propose. That makes SafeCircle a realistic "first five
minutes" layer that can later plug into 112, not a fantasy.
3.1 India: 112 / ERSS (national)
The MHA ERSS page says ERSS-112 is state-run and receives distress signals over 10
channels: voice call, SMS, SOS, email, web request, chatbot, media crawler, IoT-based
signals, WhatsApp and external signals. Calls are passed to police, health, fire, disaster,
women, children or railway dispatchers on GIS-based dispatch terminals.
Coverage conflict from v1 resolved. A Rajya Sabha answer (Dec 2023) says ERSS is
operational in all 36 states and UTs. The "24" figure is the ERSS 2.0 upgrade: 24 states
and UTs, including Tamil Nadu and Puducherry, had moved to ERSS 2.0, with the rest
expected by March 2026. Verify current ERSS 2.0 status before quoting.
112 India app: a button sends an alert with location and calls 112 (112.gov.in). Its SHOUT
feature enlists volunteers registered with the state police, aimed mainly at women
(OneIndia).
Phone triggers (from v1): dial 112; press the power button 3 times; long-press 5 or 9 on
a feature phone.
Why it matters for us: "IoT-based signals" and "external signals" are already listed
channels. Real integration is still Roadmap and needs permission, but the door exists.
3.2 Tamil Nadu and Chennai (from v1, not re-checked)
Kavalan SOS (TN Police, June 2018): red SOS button, 5-second countdown, sends
location and back-camera video to police; callback within about a minute; SMS to
registered contacts. App-based and police-only.
Kaaval Karangal (Greater Chennai Police, April 2021): helpline and rescue team for
abandoned elderly people. A service you call, not a trigger.

3.4 Gaps SafeCircle fills
1. The trigger still needs a smartphone. 112 India, the power-button shortcut and Kavalan
all assume a working phone in hand.
2. Everything is police- or ambulance-centric. Guards, RWAs, neighbours and nearby
doctors sit outside the loop.
3. Volunteer layers are narrow. SHOUT targets women; GoodSAM targets cardiac arrest
only.
4. No status loop to the person. Nobody tells a frightened senior, by touch or light, that
help is coming.
5. No building-level coverage. Nothing links to society security, gates or flats.
6. No profile-aware briefing. Every caller is treated the same.
7. No cancel verification. No system we found checks whether a cancel was made under
duress.

T1. Physical button (hero feature)
No screen, login or reading. A long press (about 2 s) starts the alert, so a brush does
not.
Two devices (Section 18): the Home button, Wi-Fi only, fixed at bed, bathroom or
wheelchair height with a large palm or elbow surface; and the Go pendant, with GPS
and 4G, worn as a pendant, wristband, bag tag or school-ID holder outside the home.
Sends its device ID and event, plus a location from the Go pendant. Name, address and
medical notes live on the server.
Full behaviour (cancel, verification) is in Section 6; feedback patterns in Section 7;

The provider pushes each message to our webhook. The backend verifies the
signature, normalises it to {source: "whatsapp", from: "+91...", text: "HELP"} ,
looks up the number, then runs the same pipeline as the button.
Location: the registered address by default. Outdoors, the reply carries a "Share
location" request.
Pricing (Verify): since 1 July 2025 Meta charges per template message. When a user
messages us, a 24-hour customer service window opens; free-form replies and utility
templates inside it are free (Meta pricing docs). Status replies to the person are
therefore free. Alerts we start ourselves to volunteers or family outside a window need
paid, pre-approved utility templates. This is one reason volunteers get a native app.
Unregistered numbers still get help, as an unverified incident: auto-reply asking for
location and one line of detail, alert to the security desk and dashboard, 112 escalation
shown. A stranger's location is not auto-sent to volunteers (abuse risk; Decision
needed).
Abuse controls: "CANCEL" reply window (then the verification call in Section 6), merge
repeat messages within a few minutes, per-number rate limit.
T3. Caregiver trigger
A caregiver's number is registered against one or more dependents. "HELP" from it raises
an alarm for the dependent. With several dependents, the bot replies with a short list, or
accepts "HELP Lakshmi". The incident is marked "raised by caregiver, location =
dependent's address" so responders do not go to the caregiver's location.
T4. SMS keyword and missed call (Roadmap)
Inbound SMS in India needs an Indian virtual-number provider; v1 notes Twilio SMS to
India is outbound-only (Verify).
A missed-call number is a familiar Indian pattern and free for the caller: ring and hang
up, the caller ID is matched against the registry. Same pipeline. Provider and cost not
yet researched.
T5. Bystander QR (new)
A small QR on the back of the pendant opens a web page with no personal data on it: "This
person may need help. Tap to alert their family." The stranger can share their phone's
location and type a note. The family and nearest guard or volunteers get an alert with that
location. It is the backup for lost children and wandering seniors when the pendant has no
GPS fix or no battery, or when the person only has a Home button. It must never show the
person's name, address or medical notes to the scanner.

5. Face A: setup and onboarding web portal
A caregiver, family member or society admin sets everything up once on a mobile web
page; the person in need never touches it. The portal pairs the button, connects it to Wi-
Fi, records the profile and runs a test, in about 10 minutes (est.).
5.1 Onboarding steps
1. Sign in with the caregiver's phone number and a one-time code.
2. Add the person (profile fields in 5.2). One caregiver can manage several people; one
person can have several caregivers.
3. Pair the button. Scan the QR on the device box. It carries the device ID and a one-time
claim code, so a stolen or second-hand button cannot be claimed by someone else. A
Go pendant also has its SIM activated here.
4. Connect the button to Wi-Fi. Two options, both browser-based:
SoftAP captive portal: the button opens its own hotspot; the phone joins it and a
setup page pops up. Works in any browser, including iPhone.
Improv Wi-Fi over Bluetooth: an open standard that sends Wi-Fi credentials over
BLE from a web page in Chrome or Edge, then redirects back to our portal to finish
onboarding (Home Assistant). Not available in iPhone browsers, which lack Web
Bluetooth.
Demo: credentials hardcoded to the phone hotspot (from v1). Several networks can
be saved and named (home, school, day centre); the one the button joins tells the
system where it is.
5. Add trigger numbers ( self , caregiver ), each confirmed by a one-time code.
6. Add family contacts in call order, and choose who may cancel an alert on the person's
behalf.
7. Choose the feedback style for the button (Section 7): standard, loud, calm, discreet or
stealth. For a Go pendant, also choose location sharing: during alerts and drills only
(default), or opt-in safe-zone mode (Roadmap).
8. Consent: the person (or guardian for a minor or a person lacking capacity) agrees to
what is stored and who sees it.
9. Run a drill. A test press goes through the whole chain marked drill : the button plays
every pattern, the caregiver gets sample messages, and no volunteer is alerted.
10. Print the kit: fridge QR (prefilled WhatsApp "HELP"), wallet card with the SafeCircle
number, and the bystander QR sticker for the pendant.

6. Button behaviour: press, cancel and verification call
A cancel never silently ends an alert. A long press cancels, the system then calls the
person to confirm, and if nobody picks up the alert is treated as real and continues. This
protects against the worst case: an attacker forcing the person to cancel.
Only one of the three outcomes ends help. Silence, a wrong answer or the duress PIN
keeps the alert alive.

v1 used a 3-5 s countdown before sending. v2 sends immediately, because the cancel-and-
verify path now handles mistakes. A pre-send countdown stays as a per-person option (0-
5 s) for people who press by accident often. Decision needed.
6.2 What happens after a cancel
1. Within about 5 s the backend places an automated verification call (IVR) to the
person's own registered phone. The caller ID shows SafeCircle.
2. The call says, in the person's language: "Your SafeCircle alert was cancelled. Press 1 if
you are safe. Press 9 if you still need help." An optional 4-digit safe PIN can be asked
after pressing 1.
3. Outcomes:
Pressed 1 (and correct PIN if set): incident closed as false alarm. Everyone gets
"Stand down, person confirmed safe". The family is told.
Pressed 9, wrong PIN, or the duress PIN: the alert continues at high priority,
flagged "possible duress". The button still shows the cancelled pattern so an
attacker is not tipped off.
No answer, busy or call rejected: one retry after about 10 s. Still nothing means the
alert is real. It resumes with every volunteer at high priority (Section 9).
4. The caregiver is told in parallel ("Lakshmi's alert was cancelled, we are checking"). A
caregiver can confirm safety too, but for high-risk profiles a caregiver's "safe" does not
stand down the guard's in-person check.
While verifying: volunteers who already accepted keep going ("Cancel requested,
verifying, continue unless stood down"). No new volunteers are added. The guard still
checks the flat, and a Go pendant keeps sending its location silently. Decision by about 60
s at most (est.).

6.4 Optional check-in call at the start
For people with a phone and no discreet or stealth flag, the same IVR can call in parallel
when an SOS starts: "Press 1 medical, 2 fall, 3 danger, 4 pressed by mistake." It never
delays dispatch. No answer means nothing changes. "4" behaves like a cancel and goes
through the verification above. The answer also seeds the volunteer flashcards (Section
14).
6.5 Provider
An Indian cloud-telephony provider such as Exotel offers outbound calls into an IVR flow
with DTMF key collection, status webhooks to our server, and a Mumbai data centre
(Exotel Voice v1 docs). It also lists Truecaller verified-business caller names and built-in
DLT compliance (Exotel services), which helps the person trust the call. Verify pricing and
KYC lead time. Demo: simulate the call with a dashboard button, or use a provider trial
account.
7. Button feedback: what the person sees, hears and feels
The button reports every stage of the response through three senses at once, so a blind
user, a deaf user and a child can each follow it. Each stage has its own colour, sound and
vibration count, so no single sense is required.

9.3 Ranking: who is "nearest"
Distance alone picks the wrong person when a nurse is 50 m further away than a student.
The ranking uses an effective ETA:
\text{score} = \text{ETA}_{\text{seconds}} - \text{skill bonus} - 
\text{reliability bonus} + \text{language or preference penalty}
ETA: from the volunteer's latest known position (Section 11) to the person's latest
location (Section 18); walking speed under 1 km, two-wheeler above, if the volunteer
said they ride one.
Skill bonus: for example 60 s for a doctor or nurse on a medical case, 30 s for a certified
first-aider (est.).
Reliability bonus: small, from past accept and arrive rates.
Preference penalty: if the profile asks for a woman responder first, or the volunteer
does not share the person's language.
Verified first: Tier 1 is taken only from verified volunteers. Unverified volunteers are
ranked within Tier 2.
The weights are guesses to be tuned with pilot data. Every dispatch decision is logged so
it can be explained later.

11.2 How the wake-up works on each platform
Android: a high-priority Firebase Cloud Messaging message is one of the allowed
exceptions for starting a foreground service from the background. A service of type
location , started by an app holding background location permission, can read
location even while the app is in the background (Android developers). So: high-
priority push, start a short location service, take one fix, send it, stop. Needs "Allow all
the time" location and the Play Console background-location declaration.
iPhone: the Location Push Service Extension (iOS 15+) is described by Apple as a
power-efficient way to query a device's location even when the app is not running,
triggered by a special location  push from our server (Apple docs). It needs a
restricted Apple entitlement and "Always" location permission. Developers report it
may not wake on some locked devices (Apple forums), so L1 remains the fallback.
Verify entitlement approval for a volunteer-safety app early.
Web app (demo): a PWA cannot read location in the background. The demo uses
location only while the app is open, and says so.
11.3 Never wait for the fresh fix
1. At 0 s the server ranks candidates from their last known zone and sends alerts at once.
2. In the same moment it sends wake-up pushes to every candidate within the radius plus
a margin.
3. Fresh fixes arrive over the next few seconds and re-rank Tier 1 (the rolling top 3).
4. A phone that does not answer keeps its old zone, shown with its age ("location 50 min
old"), and drops in rank.
This is why hourly is enough: as you noted, people usually stay in one place for a while, and
the alert-time fix corrects anyone who has moved.
11.4 Privacy rules for volunteer location
The server stores only a coarse zone (about 1 km cell) between incidents; never a
movement history.
Exact fixes are kept only for an incident and deleted shortly after it closes (for example
within 72 h; Decision needed).
Family and the person see the volunteer's name, verified badge, distance and ETA,
never a live map of the volunteer.
The volunteer can see and clear their own location log.
Spoofed GPS: detect mock-location settings, cross-check against network position;
device integrity checks (Play Integrity, App Attest) are Roadmap. Not perfect.

12.3 Community vote ("vouching")
The question is "do you know and trust this person?", not "do you like them?".
1. A new L1 volunteer requests verification for their area.
2. The system invites the society verifier (if the society has one) and up to about 15
randomly chosen L2 members living within about 1 km (est.).
3. Each voter sees only: photo, first name and initial, street or block (not flat), occupation,
and how long they have lived there. Never the ID.
4. Voters choose: I know them and vouch, I know them and have a concern (reason goes
privately to the admin), or I don't know them (abstain).
5. Pass rule (proposal): at least 3 vouches, and vouches are at least two thirds of non-
abstain votes, within 7 days. Any concern sends the case to an admin instead of auto-
rejecting. Decision needed.
6. Society verifier path: an RWA office-bearer appointed by the society admin can
approve residents and staff they know directly. This is the fallback when there are not
enough voters nearby.
7. Quorum not reached: the volunteer stays L1 and is told how to reach a verifier. They
are never shown as rejected.
Safeguards
Collusion: accounts sharing a phone, device or address cannot vouch for each other;
each voter has a monthly vouch limit; new L2 members cannot vote for 30 days.
Accountability: if a vouched volunteer is suspended for misconduct within 6 months,
the vouchers' future votes count for less.
Bias: neighbourhood votes can reflect prejudice (for example against tenants,
domestic workers, or by caste, religion or gender). Concerns need a written reason,
every rejection can be appealed, the verifier path stays open, and rejection rates are
monitored by group.

18.3 How GPS works, in plain words
Satellites and timing. A GPS receiver listens to timing signals from satellites (GPS, plus
Galileo, BeiDou, GLONASS or India's NavIC on multi-system receivers) and computes
its position from them. It needs to see the sky; roofs, walls, basements and lifts block
it.
Cold and hot start. To compute a position it needs the satellites' orbit data. Without
fresh data a cold start takes about 24 s on the u-blox MAX-M10S; with saved data a hot
start takes about 1 s (SparkFun MAX-M10S).
Backup battery. A small onboard battery keeps the clock and orbit data for up to
about five hours with main power off (same source). A pendant that had a fix recently
starts almost instantly.
Assisted GPS. The pendant can download orbit data over Wi-Fi or 4G and skip the cold
start; u-blox lists an aided start of about 1 s for its M10 chip-antenna module (SparkFun
SAM-M10Q). Firmware refreshes this data while charging.
Power. The MAX-M10S draws under 25 mW in continuous tracking, about 6-25 mA
depending on mode (SparkFun MAX-M10S). The SAM-M10Q lists about 10 mA tracking
and 5 mA in power-save mode (SparkFun SAM-M10Q). Small enough to run for a whole
incident, too much to leave on all day.
Antenna. The GPS antenna must face outward and up, away from the body and the
battery. Test on a wrist, around the neck and in a pocket.
NavIC (optional). India's own satellite system. Quectel's L89HA-S90 receives GPS and
NavIC on L1 and L5 with an integrated antenna and assisted start, and is sold in India
(Hubtronics). NavIC is required for vehicle trackers under AIS-140 (Telit); we found no
such rule for personal devices.
18.4 How the alert leaves the pendant
1. A saved Wi-Fi network (home, school) if in range: fastest and free.
2. 4G Cat-1 through the SIM everywhere else. Cat-1 runs on ordinary 4G networks. The
LilyGO T-A7670G covers global LTE bands, including B1, B3, B5, B8, B40 and B41, and
falls back to GSM (LilyGO). Verify band coverage with Jio, Airtel and Vi in the pilot
area.
3. Bluetooth to a paired phone running a background helper (Roadmap).
4. Store and retry with the red pattern if nothing works.
The LilyGO A7670 boards are data-only, with no voice calls (LilyGO). That is fine: the
pendant never needs to call, and verification calls go to the person's phone or

18.5 Power and battery
GPS stays off between alerts. That saves battery and makes the pendant a safety
device, not a tracker (Section 20.6). An opt-in safe-zone mode is Roadmap.
Current peaks. A 4G modem draws short, high current bursts while transmitting. The
MT3608 boost converter from v1 is not suitable; use the board's own power path or a
LiPo with a regulator rated for the modem's peak. Verify the peak in the modem
datasheet. LilyGO's A7670 boards are designed around an 18650 cell.
Target (est.): a charge every 3-7 days, a low-battery pattern on the pendant and a
message to the caregiver at 20%.
Charging: a magnetic or pogo-pin dock (Roadmap enclosure), so seniors and children
never fiddle with a cable.

19.4 Security
Device spoofing and replay: HMAC signature with a per-device key, plus an increasing
sequence number.
Transport: TLS everywhere in production. Plain HTTP on the hotspot is demo-only and
labelled so.
At rest: field-level encryption for medical notes, access codes, ID references and
person location fixes.
Staged disclosure enforced by the briefing endpoint, not by hiding fields in the app.
Webhooks: verify every provider signature; reject unsigned calls.
Rate limits per device, phone number and volunteer.
Masked calling so no phone numbers are exchanged.
Retention jobs delete fixes, chats and recordings on schedule (Section 20).

20.1 Data protection (DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025)
The DPDP Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 with a phased rollout; the core
duties (notice and consent, security, breach reporting, children's consent) apply 18
months later, around May 2027 (SCC Online, AMLEGALS).
Children: Rule 10 needs verifiable consent from a parent before processing a child's
data; Rule 11 needs a lawful guardian's verifiable consent for a person with a disability
(SCC Online). The portal's consent step (Section 5.1) is built for this. Many adults with
disabilities have no guardian and consent for themselves.
Design to the rules now even though the main duties start in 2027: a pilot would run
into that date.
Data minimisation: every profile field must change a response (Section 5.2).
Volunteers store a coarse zone, not a movement history (Section 11.4). The pendant's
GPS is off between alerts (Section 18.5).
Rights: download and delete for people, caregivers and volunteers; consent can be
withdrawn in the portal or app.

20.4 Volunteer legal protection
India's Good Samaritan guidelines came from the Supreme Court's 2016 SaveLife
Foundation ruling and were later written into the Motor Vehicles Act as Section 134A in
the 2019 amendment (Supreme Court order). That section protects people helping
victims of accidents involving a motor vehicle (Daily Star explainer).
Gap: most SafeCircle cases (a fall at home, a seizure, harassment) are not road
accidents, so that specific protection does not apply. Verify with a lawyer what
general-law protection exists for good-faith first aid.
Mitigations: a code of conduct (stay within training, hand over to professionals, no
driving under emergency conditions), the mission log as a record, volunteer accident
insurance for the pilot, and clear terms that volunteering is optional. GoodSAM's
approach is similar: no obligation to attend, and a code of conduct designed to keep
responders within a Good Samaritan act (NIAS FAQ).
20.5 Platform honesty
SafeCircle never promises that a volunteer will come. Every screen and the terms say it
adds to 112, it does not replace it.
Medical content is doctor-reviewed and dated (Section 13.3).
Verification calls that are recorded say so at the start.
20.6 Ethics watch-list
Community votes can carry prejudice (Section 12.3 safeguards).
Wandering geofences and daily check-ins for children and people with dementia are
surveillance; they stay Roadmap and opt-in with consent. The Go pendant is not a
tracker: by default it shares location only during an alert or a drill, and any other mode
needs the adult's own consent, or a parent's for a child.
A discreet profile must never become a way to hide abuse of the person by the person
who set it up; the "never inform" list is reviewed by an admin when it names a caregiver.

Question Answer
Isn't the pendant a tracker? Not by default. It shares location only during
an alert or a drill; any other mode needs the
adult's own consent, or a parent's for a child.
Why not GPS in every button? A wall or bedside button never moves, so
the registered address is exact and the
battery lasts far longer. GPS and 4G go only
in the pendant people wear outside.
What if a beep would put a girl in more
danger?
Stealth mode: the button confirms each
stage with light only, with no sound, no
vibration and no calls to her phone, so
people nearby cannot tell help is coming.
